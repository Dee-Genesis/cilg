About CILG

The Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance

A registered professional body advancing world-class standards in governance, leadership development, and professional excellence

Section 1 — Institute Overview

Who We Are

A professional body at the intersection of leadership, governance, and institutional excellence — established in the United States of America with a global mandate to advance professional excellence in leadership and governance practice.

CILG

Our Founding Conviction

The Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance (CILG) was founded on a foundational conviction: that the quality of governance and leadership in organisations — whether corporations, government agencies, non-profits, educational institutions, or international bodies — directly determines the welfare of the people they serve. Poor governance costs lives, destroys wealth, undermines democratic institutions, and erodes public trust.

Excellent governance, by contrast, creates the conditions for sustainable development, institutional resilience, and human flourishing. CILG exists to make excellent governance more common, more accessible, and more rigorously defined.

Four Primary Functions

As a professional body, CILG performs four primary functions that distinguish it from academic institutions, training providers, and trade associations:

🎓
Credentialing
Awarding recognised professional qualifications and designations that validate governance competence.
⚖️
Standard-Setting
Establishing and maintaining the professional and ethical standards of the governance profession.
🔬
Knowledge Advancement
Producing and disseminating research and professional scholarship that advances governance practice.
📢

Representing the governance profession in public policy debates and regulatory consultations.

Section 1.2 — Historical Development

Founding Principles & Development

CILG was established in response to a documented and persistent gap in the professional formation of governance practitioners — drawing on the institutional models of the world's most respected professional bodies.

Across industries and sectors, decision-makers at board level, executive level, and in senior governance roles frequently lack the formal professional preparation that their responsibilities demand. Unlike the legal, medical, and accounting professions — which have long maintained rigorous credentialing systems — governance and leadership have historically been practised by individuals whose professional development was incidental to career advancement rather than systematically structured around a recognised body of knowledge and ethical standards.

The founders of CILG drew on the institutional models of the world's most respected professional bodies — including ACCA, CIPD, ICSA, IoD, and CMI — to design a qualification and membership framework that would bring equivalent professional rigour to the governance and leadership profession.

①
Founding
Establishment and Initial Registration
CILG is established and registered as a professional body under the laws of the United States of America, with initial registrations in New Mexico, Alaska, and Michigan. The founding Board of Governors convenes and approves the inaugural Qualification Framework and Code of Professional Conduct.
②
Qualification Framework
Launch of the Five-Level Qualification Framework
CILG launches its comprehensive five-level qualification framework — from the Foundation Certificate (Level 1) to the Chartered Professional designation (Level 5) — covering 25 modules with rigorous assessment standards aligned to international professional body benchmarks.
③
Membership Framework
Membership Grade Structure and Professional Community
The seven-grade membership structure is established, from Student Member (SCILG) through to Fellow and Honorary Fellow (FCILG/HonFCILG), with a Corporate Membership tier. The CILG Members' Portal and CPD framework are launched.
④
Certification Programmes
Launch of 16 Professional Certification Programmes
CILG launches 16 professional certification programmes spanning AI Governance, Human Resource Management, Strategic Leadership, Public Administration, Project Management, Risk Management, ESG, Data Governance, and others — making CILG's professional development accessible across specialist domains.
⑤
Global Reach
International Presence and Mutual Recognition
CILG establishes Mutual Recognition Agreements (MRAs) with peer professional bodies, launches the Accredited Learning Partner (ALP) network, and extends its professional community to members across more than 40 countries.
Section 2 — Legal Status & Registration

Legal Status, Jurisdiction & Registration

CILG's legal framework reflects its commitment to transparency, accountability, and proper governance of its own affairs — embodying in its institutional structure the very standards it promotes in the profession.

The Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance is a registered professional body established and operating under the laws of the United States of America. CILG is constituted as a non-profit professional association — an institution whose governing purpose is the advancement of the profession and the public interest, not the generation of financial returns for shareholders or owners. All surplus revenues generated by CILG's activities are reinvested into its professional development, research, and advocacy missions.

CILG's legal personality, governance structure, and operational authority derive from its constitutional documents — the Articles of Incorporation and Bylaws — which vest supreme authority in the Board of Governors, a body of elected Fellows and appointed independent members who are collectively accountable for the direction, standards, and integrity of the Institute.

🏔️
New Mexico
Primary Registration
CILG is registered in the State of New Mexico as a professional body and non-profit association. New Mexico serves as CILG's principal state of registration, with the Institute maintaining its registered agent and primary corporate records in compliance with the New Mexico Non-Profit Corporation Act.
🌲
Alaska
Active Registration
CILG maintains active registration in the State of Alaska, extending its legal authority to operate, contract, and credential professionals in the Alaskan jurisdiction. This registration supports CILG's service to governance and leadership professionals in Alaska and the broader Pacific region.
🌊
Michigan
Active Registration
CILG is registered in the State of Michigan, establishing its legal presence in the Great Lakes region and the Midwest. Michigan's active professional community and strong public sector presence make it a significant operational jurisdiction for CILG's programmes and membership activities.

📋 Legal and Regulatory Compliance

  • CILG maintains full compliance with the registration, reporting, and governance requirements of each state of registration and files all required annual reports and disclosures on schedule.
  • As a non-profit professional body, CILG operates in accordance with applicable federal and state non-profit laws, including IRS requirements for tax-exempt organisations.
  • CILG's financial records are audited annually by an independent external auditor. The audited financial statements are available to members in good standing on written request to the Secretary-General.
  • CILG does not engage in partisan political activity. Its advocacy activities are confined to evidence-based policy engagement on matters directly relevant to governance standards, professional education, and the public interest.
  • CILG's operations, examinations, and credentialing activities are governed by its own Qualification Framework and Membership Framework — documents reviewed and approved by the Board of Governors on a biennial basis.
Section 3 — Vision & Mission

Vision and Mission Statements

CILG's vision and mission are not aspirational slogans — they are operational commitments that drive every programme we design, every standard we set, and every professional we develop.

Our Vision
The Destination We Are Building Towards
"To be the foremost global authority and most trusted professional body in the fields of leadership and governance — recognized for cultivating principled, competent, and transformative leaders who drive sustainable development, institutional excellence, and societal progress across all sectors and geographies."
Our Mission
How We Create Value Every Day
"To advance the theory and practice of ethical leadership and sound governance through world-class professional education, rigorous credentialing, impactful research, and evidence-based advocacy — empowering individuals, organisations, and institutions to operate with excellence, accountability, transparency, and purpose."

Interpreting Our Vision

The vision statement articulates CILG's highest aspiration — to be the foremost global authority in governance and leadership. This is a standard against which we hold ourselves rigorously accountable. It means that our qualification framework must be benchmarked against the world's best; that our research must advance the frontier of professional knowledge; that our membership community must uphold the highest standards of ethical conduct; and that our voice in governance policy must be informed by evidence and respected by regulators and institutions.

The reference to all sectors and geographies reflects CILG's conviction that the governance profession's challenges are universal, transcending industry, sector, and national context.

Interpreting Our Mission

The mission statement identifies four mechanisms through which CILG fulfils its purpose: professional education (the qualification and certification programmes through which we develop professional competence); rigorous credentialing (the assessment and membership framework through which we validate and recognise that competence); impactful research (the scholarly and professional inquiry through which we advance governance knowledge); and evidence-based advocacy (our engagement with policymakers and regulators on behalf of the governance profession and the public interest).

Together, these four mechanisms constitute CILG's complete institutional contribution to the governance profession and the public interest.

Section 6 — Core Values & Ethics

Core Values and Ethical Framework

CILG's values are not merely aspirational — they are operationalised standards against which CILG holds itself, its staff, and all its members accountable. They underpin every institutional decision, every professional standard, and every interaction with the professional community.

⚖️
Integrity
CILG maintains absolute honesty and transparency in all its activities — in the accuracy of its communications, the rigour of its assessments, the independence of its governance, and the courage to uphold standards even when doing so is inconvenient or commercially costly.
🏆
Excellence
CILG is committed to the highest achievable standard in everything it does — from the intellectual quality of its qualifications and the rigour of its assessments, to the professionalism of its member services and the calibre of its research and publications. Good enough is never good enough.
🛡️
Accountability
CILG holds itself to the same accountability standards it promotes in the profession. Its Board of Governors, Secretariat, and Committee members are individually and collectively accountable for the Institute's performance, standards, and use of resources — publicly and transparently.
🔍
Transparency
CILG operates with maximum feasible transparency in its governance, its finances, its standards, and its decision-making. Members, prospective members, employers, and the public should be able to understand how CILG makes decisions, deploys resources, and upholds its standards.
🌍
Inclusivity
CILG is committed to making world-class governance education and professional recognition accessible to every qualified professional regardless of geography, socioeconomic background, gender, ethnicity, disability, or organisational sector. Governance excellence should not be the exclusive domain of the privileged.
💡
Innovation
CILG actively seeks new and better ways to fulfil its mission — in the design and delivery of its qualifications, the application of technology to professional education, the development of new governance frameworks for emerging challenges, and the advancement of governance research methodologies.
🌱
Purpose
Every decision CILG makes is guided by its ultimate purpose: the advancement of the public interest through excellent governance and leadership. Commercial considerations, reputational pressures, and institutional convenience are always subordinated to this purpose.
🤝
Stewardship
CILG understands its role as a steward of the governance profession — not its owner. The Institute serves the profession and the public interest; it does not define them. This stewardship orientation informs CILG's humility in standard-setting and its obligation to develop the next generation of governance professionals.
Section 6.2 — Ethical Framework

The Code of Professional Conduct

CILG's ethical framework is grounded in the seven principles of the CILG Code of Professional Conduct — the binding ethical standard to which all members of CILG are held. These principles are not abstract ideals; they are enforceable professional obligations that define what it means to be a CILG member in good standing.

Principle Description and Application
Integrity Being honest, truthful, and transparent in all professional dealings — rejecting dishonesty, deception, and misrepresentation in any form. For governance professionals, this means providing boards and stakeholders with complete, accurate, and appropriately presented information at all times.
Professional Competence Maintaining the level of professional knowledge and skill required for the responsibilities undertaken — through continuous professional development, honest self-assessment, and the willingness to seek support when facing challenges beyond current competence.
Accountability Accepting responsibility for professional decisions and their consequences — not deflecting accountability to others or to circumstances. Governance professionals have a specific accountability obligation to the stakeholders they serve.
Transparency Proactively sharing information that stakeholders need to make informed decisions — not selectively withholding information to protect professional or organisational interests at the expense of those stakeholders.
Respect & Inclusivity Treating all people — regardless of background, identity, or organisational position — with dignity and respect. Actively promoting inclusion in governance bodies and the organisations they govern.
Professional Independence Maintaining the independence of professional judgment that governance roles require — resisting undue pressure from executives, major shareholders, or other powerful stakeholders to compromise professional assessments or advice.
Stewardship Exercising governance responsibilities as a steward of the interests of all legitimate stakeholders — not as an agent of the most powerful, the most vocal, or those most able to reward or harm the governance professional personally.

🏅

📏
Standard-Setting

🔬
Knowledge Advancement

Education and Capacity Building

Education, Training, and Capacity Development

CILG delivers world-class education and training through a multi-level qualification framework, specialist certifications, and capacity development programmes serving governance professionals at all career stages.

The Five-Level Qualification Framework

CILG's qualification structure provides a clear, progressive pathway from foundational principles to mastery-level expertise in governance and leadership. Each level builds on previous knowledge, increasing in scope, complexity, and professional application.

Level 1: Foundation Certificate in Leadership and Governance

An accessible entry-level qualification for professionals beginning their governance journey. Covers foundational governance concepts, leadership principles, organisational accountability, and the role of governance in institutional effectiveness.

Level 2: Certificate in Leadership and Governance

Builds on foundational knowledge with deeper exploration of governance frameworks, board dynamics, stakeholder management, professional ethics, and governance in specific sectors.

Level 3: Advanced Certificate in Leadership and Governance

A professional-grade qualification for practitioners seeking to deepen their expertise. Covers strategic governance, risk oversight, financial stewardship, regulatory environments, and governance in complex organisations.

Level 4: Diploma in Leadership and Governance

A comprehensive diploma integrating governance theory with institutional practice. Focuses on board effectiveness, ESG governance, corporate strategy, institutional governance models, and leadership in diverse contexts.

Level 5: Advanced Diploma in Leadership and Governance

The flagship CILG qualification and the highest level of professional credential. A master-practitioner programme for senior governance professionals. Covers advanced governance challenges, systemic accountability, transformational leadership, global governance issues, and evidence-based governance research.

Delivery and Learning Methods

CILG qualifications are delivered globally through a network of Accredited Learning Partners and in collaboration with universities, professional associations, and training institutions. Learning modes include in-person instructor-led programmes, hybrid delivery combining online and face-to-face learning, self-paced online study, and blended learning approaches. Delivery is tailored to the needs of learners and the contexts in which they work, ensuring accessibility and relevance across regions and professional settings.

Assessment and Credentialing

All CILG qualifications are assessed through rigorous, standardised assessment methods including written examinations, case study analysis, reflective practice assignments, and professional portfolio development. Assessment is designed to evaluate both theoretical knowledge and practical application of governance principles. Successful completion of any CILG qualification results in the award of a recognised credential, providing independent verification of the holder's governance knowledge and competence.

Capacity Development Programmes

Beyond formal qualifications, CILG delivers targeted capacity development programmes to strengthen governance capacity in specific institutions, sectors, and regions. These programmes are tailored to address identified governance challenges and support institutions in meeting their specific governance objectives.

Research and Knowledge Support

CILG maintains a comprehensive knowledge resource on governance practice, policy, and trends. Members have access to CILG research publications, policy briefs, case studies, and governance resources to support their continuing professional development and practice.
