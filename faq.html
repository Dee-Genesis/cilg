About CILG
5 questions
What is the Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance (CILG)?

The Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance (CILG) is a globally recognised professional body dedicated to developing outstanding leaders and governance professionals. Registered in the United States (New Mexico, Alaska, and Michigan), CILG advances leadership and governance practice through internationally accredited qualifications, rigorous professional standards, structured membership pathways, and a growing global community of practitioners.

CILG's mission is to uphold the highest standards of professional conduct and competence in leadership and governance across all sectors — public, private, and non-profit.

Where is CILG registered and recognised?

CILG is a registered professional body incorporated in the United States of America, with registration spanning New Mexico, Alaska, and Michigan. Its global secretariat is located at 2222 W. Grand River Ave STE A, Okemos, MI 48864, USA.

CILG operates internationally and its designations and qualifications are recognised by member organisations and institutions across multiple jurisdictions worldwide.

What are the core objectives of CILG?

CILG's core objectives include: promoting excellence in leadership and governance practice globally; establishing and maintaining rigorous professional standards and a code of ethics; providing internationally recognised qualifications and credentials; building a global network of governance and leadership professionals; supporting Continuing Professional Development (CPD); and advancing research and knowledge in the fields of leadership and governance.

For the full objectives framework, visit the Objectives page.

Who governs CILG?

CILG is governed by its Board of Governors, which provides strategic oversight and ensures the Institute operates in accordance with its charter, values, and professional standards. The Board includes distinguished professionals from diverse sectors across multiple countries.

CILG also appoints Country Directors to represent the Institute's interests and coordinate activities in specific regions. You can view the full governing structure on the Board of Governors and Country Directors pages.

How can I contact CILG?

You can reach CILG through the following channels:

  • General enquiries: info@cilgglobal.org
  • Admissions: admissions@cilgglobal.org
  • Membership: membership@cilgglobal.org

Office hours are Monday – Friday, 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (ET). Visit the Contact page for more options.

Membership
8 questions
What membership grades does CILG offer?

CILG offers a structured, progressive membership framework with the following grades and designatory letters:

  • Student Member — for those currently enrolled in a CILG qualification programme
  • Associate Member (ACILG) — entry-level professional membership
  • Member (MCILG) — full professional membership
  • Senior Member (SMCILG) — for experienced governance professionals
  • Fellow (FCILG) — the highest individual membership grade, recognising distinguished contribution

Each grade carries specific designatory letters that members are entitled to use after their names. Visit the Membership Framework page for full entry criteria.

What are the eligibility requirements for CILG membership?

Eligibility varies by membership grade. In general, applicants are assessed against a combination of:

  • Academic qualifications (relevant degrees, diplomas, or CILG qualifications)
  • Professional experience in leadership, governance, or related disciplines
  • Commitment to CILG's Code of Ethics and professional standards
  • Continuing Professional Development (CPD) engagement (for senior grades)

Full grade-specific criteria are detailed on the Membership Framework page. If you are unsure which grade applies to you, contact membership@cilgglobal.org for guidance.

How do I apply for CILG membership?

Applications are submitted through the CILG Application Portal. The process involves:

  • Selecting the appropriate membership grade
  • Completing the online application form
  • Uploading supporting documents (certificates, CV, references)
  • Paying the applicable application fee

Once submitted, your application will be reviewed by the Membership Committee. Successful applicants will receive a formal acceptance letter along with their digital certificate and membership number. Start your application at the Application Portal.

What designatory letters can I use after becoming a member?

CILG members are entitled to use the following post-nominal designatory letters corresponding to their membership grade:

  • Associate Member: ACILG
  • Member: MCILG
  • Senior Member: SMCILG
  • Fellow: FCILG

These designatory letters are internationally recognised marks of professional distinction in leadership and governance. Members must maintain their membership in good standing to continue using them.

Can I upgrade my membership grade over time?

Yes. CILG's membership framework is designed as a progressive career pathway. Members are encouraged to advance through grades as their professional experience, qualifications, and contributions to the field grow. Upgrade applications follow the same assessment process as initial applications and require demonstration of the competencies and criteria associated with the target grade.

Contact membership@cilgglobal.org to discuss your upgrade eligibility.

What are the benefits of CILG membership?

CILG membership offers a wide range of professional benefits, including:

  • Internationally recognised designatory letters
  • Access to CILG's global professional network
  • CPD resources, webinars, and governance knowledge tools
  • Listing in the CILG Member Directory
  • Access to CILG publications and research insights
  • Eligibility to sit for CILG professional certification examinations
  • Priority access to CILG events, conferences, and forums
  • Professional recognition and credibility in leadership and governance roles
Is CILG membership renewable? How does renewal work?

Yes, CILG membership is subject to annual renewal. Renewal requires members to: pay the annual subscription fee for their membership grade; demonstrate completion of their CPD obligations for the year; and confirm their continued adherence to CILG's Code of Ethics and Professional Standards.

Members who fail to renew may lose their right to use CILG designatory letters until membership is reinstated.

What is the Supplementary Standard Framework?

The Supplementary Standard Framework provides additional pathways and criteria for membership assessment beyond the primary academic and experiential requirements. It accommodates professionals whose backgrounds involve non-traditional routes to leadership and governance excellence — for example, those with extensive community governance roles, executive leadership in specialised sectors, or significant contributions to governance through research and publication.

Full details are available on the Membership page.

Qualifications
6 questions
What qualifications does CILG offer?

CILG operates a five-level Qualification Framework in Leadership and Governance, designed as a progressive academic and professional pathway:

  • Level 1: Foundation Certificate in Leadership & Governance
  • Level 2: Certificate in Leadership & Governance
  • Level 3: Diploma in Leadership & Governance
  • Level 4: Advanced Diploma in Leadership & Governance
  • Level 5: Postgraduate Diploma / Strategic Leadership & Governance

Each qualification builds competence progressively from foundational principles through to strategic mastery. See the Qualification Framework for full programme outlines.

Are CILG qualifications internationally recognised?

Yes. CILG qualifications are designed to meet international standards in professional education and are recognised across CILG's global membership network. As CILG continues to grow its partnerships and formal recognition agreements with institutions and regulatory bodies worldwide, the standing of its qualifications is continually strengthened.

Employers and institutions recognising CILG qualifications span multiple countries across Africa, North America, Europe, and Asia.

What are the entry requirements for CILG qualifications?

Entry requirements differ by qualification level. Generally:

  • Levels 1–2 are accessible to individuals with secondary education or foundational professional experience
  • Levels 3–4 require prior qualifications at a lower level or relevant professional experience
  • Level 5 typically requires a bachelor's degree or equivalent, plus relevant professional experience

CILG also considers the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) for candidates with substantial professional experience but without formal academic prerequisites. Full entry criteria are on the Qualifications page.

How are CILG qualifications assessed?

Assessment methodologies vary by level and programme but typically include a combination of: written assignments and case studies; examinations (online or invigilated); reflective professional portfolios; and project or research work at higher levels. All assessments are designed to evaluate both theoretical knowledge and practical application of leadership and governance principles.

What is the Professional Standard Framework for qualifications?

The Professional Standard Framework (PSF) defines the competencies, knowledge, and professional behaviours expected at each qualification level. It ensures that CILG qualifications map to real-world governance and leadership roles and that graduates are equipped with measurable, practitioner-relevant skills.

The PSF is integral to both programme design and assessment. More information is available on the Qualifications page.

Do CILG qualifications lead automatically to membership?

Completion of a CILG qualification is a strong basis for membership application and may satisfy the academic criteria for specific membership grades. However, membership requires a separate application and assessment, including evaluation of professional experience, ethical commitment, and CPD engagement. Qualification holders are encouraged to apply for the membership grade appropriate to their overall professional profile.

Professional Certification
6 questions
What professional certifications does CILG award?

CILG awards professional certifications that validate specific competencies in leadership and governance. These are distinct from CILG's academic qualifications and are designed to recognise practitioners who have demonstrated mastery in defined areas of practice. Certifications include specialised credentials tied to governance domains such as board leadership, public sector governance, corporate governance, and ethical leadership.

Full details of available certifications are on the Professional Certification page.

What is the difference between a CILG qualification and a CILG certification?

CILG qualifications are structured academic programmes (Levels 1–5) that provide a comprehensive educational pathway in leadership and governance, building knowledge and skills progressively.

CILG certifications are competency-based credentials that validate demonstrated professional expertise in specific domains. They are generally shorter, assessment-focused, and targeted at practitioners with existing experience who wish to gain formal recognition in a particular area of practice.

Both qualifications and certifications can support membership applications and career advancement.

Who is eligible to apply for CILG professional certifications?

Eligibility for CILG certifications typically requires: current CILG membership (at Associate level or above); relevant professional experience in the certification's domain; and, in some cases, completion of a prerequisite qualification or programme. Specific eligibility criteria are listed for each certification on the certifications page.

How are professional certifications assessed?

Assessment for CILG professional certifications is competency-based and may include: structured examinations; submission of a professional practice portfolio; case study analysis; and peer or panel review. Assessment criteria are aligned to CILG's Professional Standards Framework to ensure rigour and real-world relevance.

Do CILG certifications expire?

CILG certifications are subject to periodic renewal to ensure that certified professionals remain current with evolving governance and leadership standards. Renewal typically requires evidence of ongoing CPD activity relevant to the certified domain. Specific renewal timelines and requirements are provided to each certification holder upon award.

How can I verify a CILG certification?

CILG maintains a public Certification Verification Portal. Employers, institutions, and other parties can verify the authenticity of any CILG certification or membership by using the Verification Portal. Guidance on the verification process is also available on the Certification Verification Guidelines page.

Governance & Professional Standards
5 questions
What is CILG's Code of Ethics?

CILG's Code of Ethics sets out the core principles of professional conduct expected of all members, certification holders, and representatives of the Institute. These principles include: integrity and honesty in all professional dealings; accountability to stakeholders and the public; transparency in governance processes; respect for persons and diversity; and commitment to continuous professional development.

All members are required to uphold the Code as a condition of membership. Breach of the Code may result in disciplinary action, including suspension or revocation of membership.

What is CILG's Professional Standards Framework?

CILG's Professional Standards Framework (PSF) defines the knowledge, competencies, and behaviours expected of leadership and governance professionals at each stage of their career. It underpins CILG's qualifications, membership criteria, and professional certification assessments, ensuring all CILG-recognised professionals meet a consistent, high standard of practice.

The PSF is structured around core competency domains including strategic leadership, governance architecture, ethical accountability, stakeholder management, and organisational resilience.

How does CILG handle complaints and disciplinary matters?

CILG operates a formal complaints and disciplinary process to investigate allegations of professional misconduct against members or certification holders. Complaints are reviewed by the appropriate committee in accordance with CILG's governance procedures, ensuring fairness, confidentiality, and due process. Outcomes may range from formal warnings to suspension or permanent revocation of membership.

To lodge a complaint or enquire about the disciplinary process, contact info@cilgglobal.org.

Does CILG engage in governance research and policy?

Yes. CILG is committed to advancing the body of knowledge in leadership and governance. The Institute publishes research insights, policy briefings, and governance guidance documents. CILG also collaborates with partner institutions on research initiatives and contributes to governance policy discourse at national and international levels.

Contact research@cilgglobal.org for information on research collaboration or access to CILG publications.

Can organisations become partners or affiliates of CILG?

Yes. CILG actively develops partnerships with universities, professional bodies, corporations, and government institutions. Partnership arrangements may include programme accreditation, training delivery, membership pathway agreements, and joint research. Interested organisations should contact partnerships@cilgglobal.org to discuss available partnership models.

Verification & CPD
4 questions
How can employers verify a CILG member's credentials?

Employers, institutions, and third parties can verify CILG credentials — including membership status, certification, and qualification awards — through the official CILG Verification Portal. The portal provides real-time confirmation of a credential holder's status and the authenticity of their CILG-issued documents.

Guidance on using the verification system is available on the Certification Verification Guidelines page.

What is CPD and why is it required for CILG members?

Continuing Professional Development (CPD) refers to the ongoing learning and professional activities that members undertake to maintain and enhance their knowledge, skills, and professional competence. CILG requires all members to engage in CPD as a condition of membership renewal, reflecting the Institute's commitment to lifelong professional growth and the currency of governance practice.

CPD activities recognised by CILG include formal training, conferences, workshops, peer learning, published research, mentoring, and governance roles. Members must log and evidence their CPD activities annually.

How many CPD hours are required per year?

CILG's CPD requirements vary by membership grade. Higher membership grades typically carry greater CPD obligations reflecting the increased professional responsibilities associated with senior designations. Specific annual CPD hour requirements for each grade are outlined in the CILG Membership Framework and the Professional Standards Framework documentation.

For grade-specific CPD requirements, refer to the Membership Framework or contact membership@cilgglobal.org.

What happens if I cannot meet my CPD requirements in a given year?

CILG recognises that exceptional circumstances — such as serious illness, bereavement, or major life events — may sometimes prevent members from fulfilling their CPD obligations in full. In such cases, members should proactively contact CILG to request a CPD deferral or relief arrangement. Applications are considered on a case-by-case basis. Members should not allow their CPD obligations to lapse without notifying the Institute, as this may affect membership standing. Contact membership@cilgglobal.org for assistance.
