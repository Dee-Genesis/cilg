About Our Governance

Governance Statement

The Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance (CILG) is a professional body registered in the United States of America, dedicated to advancing the discipline and practice of ethical leadership and sound governance across all sectors. CILG operates under a governance framework consistent with best practices for professional bodies and learned societies, ensuring the Institute remains accountable to its members, its profession, and the wider public.

Our governance structure comprises an experienced Executive Leadership team supported by a distinguished Advisory Board, a system of standing committees, and clearly defined policies governing conduct, integrity, and professional standards.

These arrangements ensure that strategic guidance, institutional oversight, and professional stewardship are exercised with rigour and transparency. All governance decisions are taken in accordance with CILG's Charter, Bylaws, and applicable US nonprofit and professional body governance standards.

The Institute holds an Annual General Meeting open to all members in good standing, who are entitled to receive the annual report, audited accounts, and to exercise voting rights as prescribed in the Bylaws.

Our Foundations

Governance Pillars

Every aspect of CILG's governance is designed around four foundational principles that guide every decision, every process, and every relationship.

🛡️
Accountability
Clear lines of responsibility for all officers, advisors, and committee members. Every role has defined duties and is subject to formal appraisal and oversight.
👁️
Transparency
Governance decisions, policies, and outcomes are communicated openly to members. Key documents are published and accessible to all members and the public.
⚡
Independence
Independent advisors ensure decisions serve the mission, free from conflicts of interest. At least two independent members sit on the Advisory Board at all times.
⚖️
Integrity
The Code of Conduct applies without exception to every person acting on behalf of CILG. Conflicts of interest are declared and managed formally.

Advisory Board

The CILG Advisory Board is constituted in accordance with the Institute's Charter and Bylaws. It provides strategic guidance and expert oversight to the Executive Leadership, drawing on the expertise of distinguished professionals from leadership, governance, law, finance, and public administration.

Independence requirement: At all times, not fewer than two members of the Advisory Board shall be independent — having no material relationship with the Institute other than their advisory role. Independent members may not chair the Finance or Audit committees.

Advisory Board members serve three-year terms, renewable for a maximum of two consecutive terms. Appointments are made by resolution of the Advisory Board following recommendation by the Governance and Nominations Committee.

Standing Committees

Committee Structure

The Advisory Board discharges its oversight responsibilities through five standing committees, each operating under written terms of reference approved by the Advisory Board. Committees may include non-Advisory Board members co-opted for their specialist expertise.

01
Audit and Risk Committee
Oversees financial reporting integrity, internal controls, external audit appointment, and enterprise risk management. Chaired by an independent advisor.
02
Governance and Nominations Committee
Reviews Advisory Board composition, manages succession planning, oversees director and officer appraisals, and ensures ongoing compliance with governance best practices.
03
Professional Standards and Ethics Committee
Upholds CILG's professional charter, administers the Code of Conduct, manages member disciplinary proceedings, and reviews certification standards.
04
Membership and Engagement Committee
Develops membership growth strategy, oversees member welfare, manages stakeholder engagement, and governs CILG's international chapter network.
05
Finance and Investment Committee
Reviews annual budgets, investment policies, financial sustainability planning, and recommends resource allocation priorities to the Advisory Board.
How We Govern

Governance Principles & Practices

These binding practices govern how CILG conducts its affairs, makes decisions, and remains accountable to its members and the wider public.

The Advisory Board meets no fewer than four times per calendar year. Additional extraordinary meetings are convened as required by the Chair or by majority request.
A quorum for Advisory Board meetings is a majority of serving members. No substantive decisions are taken without a quorum.
All Advisory Board members and officers complete an annual declaration of interests. A conflict of interest register is maintained and reviewed at every Advisory Board meeting.
CILG's governance framework and governing documents are reviewed against applicable US nonprofit and professional body governance standards at least every three years.
Formal minutes of all Advisory Board and committee meetings are maintained. Members in good standing may request access to approved minutes in accordance with the Institute's information policy.
The Institute holds an Annual General Meeting open to all members in good standing, who are entitled to receive the annual report, audited accounts, and to exercise voting rights as prescribed in the Bylaws.
CILG publishes its key governance documents — including the Charter, Bylaws, and Code of Conduct — and makes them available to members and the public.
All persons acting on behalf of CILG are bound by the Code of Conduct. Breaches are investigated independently and may result in removal from office or membership.
Accountability & Reporting

Annual Reporting

CILG is committed to transparent reporting of its governance, financial performance, and strategic outcomes. The Institute publishes an Annual Report following each financial year, presented to members at the AGM.

The report includes audited financial statements, a review of strategic progress, a governance compliance statement, and a summary of Advisory Board activities.

The Institute's accounts are independently audited by a qualified external auditor appointed by the Advisory Board on the recommendation of the Audit and Risk Committee.
