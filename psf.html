Professional Standards Framework

The comprehensive standards, eligibility requirements, assessment methodology, and delivery policies governing all CILG qualification programmes.

1. Framework Overview & Purpose

The CILG Professional Standards Framework (hereafter "the Framework") articulates the minimum requirements for the design, delivery, and assessment of qualifications offered by the Chartered Institute of Leadership and Governance. These standards apply to all learning programmes regardless of mode of delivery: in-person, online, blended, or distance learning.

1.1 Scope of the Framework

This Framework establishes standards for:

  • Programme eligibility and entry requirements — minimum academic and experiential thresholds for entry to programmes at each level
  • Assessment design and methodology — examination standards, grading criteria, and marking protocols
  • Delivery modes and quality assurance — pedagogical standards, tutor competency, and learning environment expectations
  • Candidate support and welfare — duty of care, reasonable adjustments, wellbeing resources
  • Programme completion and time limits — maximum duration for programme completion from first registration
  • Examiner standards and marking integrity — qualification and training requirements for examiners; moderation and quality assurance protocols
  • Appeals and feedback mechanisms — candidate right to review, appeal, and feedback processes

1.2 Core Principles

The Framework is underpinned by the following principles:

  • Competency-focused assessment: Assessments measure demonstrated professional knowledge, skills, and behaviours aligned to defined competency standards
  • Fairness and transparency: Assessment criteria, grading standards, and candidate rights are clearly published and consistently applied
  • Proportionality: Entry requirements, assessment load, and time limits are appropriate to qualification level and professional context
  • Accessibility: Qualifications are delivered and assessed in ways that are accessible to all candidates, with reasonable adjustments provided as needed
  • Continuous improvement: Assessment design and delivery are regularly reviewed and updated based on candidate feedback and educational research

1.3 Duty of Care & Accessibility

CILG is committed to supporting the success of all candidates. Candidates experiencing personal difficulty, disability, or additional learning needs are encouraged to contact the Accessibility Team (accessibility@cilg.org) as early as possible. Reasonable adjustments will be considered in accordance with the CILG Reasonable Adjustments Policy. Examples include:

  • Extended time (up to 50% additional time for written examinations)
  • Separate invigilation room
  • Large print or Braille examination papers
  • Use of assistive technology or reader/scribe support
  • Postponement or deferral of examination to a later window

Requests must be submitted with supporting documentation no later than 8 weeks before the examination date.

2. The Four Pillars of Professional Excellence

CILG's qualifications are structured around four pillars, each defining a discrete competency domain and assessment model:

2.1 Pillar 1: Knowledge & Comprehension

Focus: Conceptual understanding, knowledge retention, and analytical ability.

Assessment Method: Multiple-choice questions (MCQ), short-answer questions, and case study analysis.

Grading: Pass (≥50%), Merit (≥65%), Distinction (≥80%).

Duration: 2–3 hours written examination; 90–180 minutes.

2.2 Pillar 2: Professional Skills & Practice

Focus: Application of knowledge to professional context, decision-making, and practical problem-solving.

Assessment Method: Scenario-based case studies, constructed-response tasks, and integrated case studies (ICS).

Grading: Pass (≥50%), Merit (≥65%), Distinction (≥80%).

Duration: 3–4 hours written examination; extended time permitted for reading comprehension.

2.3 Pillar 3: Work-Based & Reflective Practice

Focus: Evidence of professional competency in the workplace, reflective learning, and continuous development.

Assessment Method: Work-based assignments (WBA), professional portfolios, reflective essays, and workplace observation.

Grading: Pass (≥50%), Merit (≥65%), Distinction (≥80%).

Submission Deadline: Assignments submitted within 30 days of window close; late submission penalties apply (5% deduction per week, max 3 weeks).

2.4 Pillar 4: Professional Integration & Capstone

Focus: Integration of all four pillars; demonstration of professional readiness and strategic thinking.

Assessment Method: Integrated case study (ICS), professional project, or viva voce (professional discussion).

Grading: Pass (≥50%), Merit (≥65%), Distinction (≥80%).

Duration: 4–5 hours for written ICS; 45–60 minutes for viva voce.

3. Qualification Levels & Eligibility

CILG qualifications are offered at five levels, aligned to the UK Qualifications Framework (CQFW) and international professional standards. Each level has defined entry requirements, assessment models, and professional outcomes.

3.2 Experience Recognition & Exemptions

Candidates with significant prior professional experience, relevant qualifications from recognised international professional bodies, or prior CILG study may be eligible for exemptions from certain modules or assessments. Exemptions are awarded on a case-by-case basis and are subject to:

  • Review by the Exemptions Committee within 10 working days of application
  • Equivalence of learning outcomes and assessment standards
  • Submission of certified evidence (qualification certificates, transcripts, professional references)
  • Payment of exemption assessment fee (typically £35–£75 per module)

For details, see the CILG Exemptions Policy.

4. Assessment Standards & Candidate Support

4.1 Assessment Design Principles

All assessments are designed in accordance with the following principles:

  • Validity: Assessments measure what they are intended to measure; assessment tasks align directly to learning outcomes and competency standards
  • Reliability: Assessments are marked consistently using detailed marking guidance; double-marking and moderation protocols ensure consistency across markers and cohorts
  • Fairness: Assessment conditions are the same for all candidates; reasonable adjustments are provided where needed to remove barriers
  • Sufficiency: Each level requires multiple assessments across all four pillars; no single assessment determines overall level award
  • Authenticity: Assessment tasks reflect real professional scenarios; work-based assignments draw on actual workplace experience
